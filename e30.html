Motor

Engines

The E30 range ran on three engine families: the veteran M10 four, the small-block M40/M42 fours that replaced it, and the belt-driven M20 "baby six" — the engine that gave the 325i its reputation. All of them sit longitudinally, canted 30° to the right, driving the rear wheels the way nature intended.

Getriebe

Manual E30s got Getrag's small-case boxes: the 240 behind the fours and the 320i, the stronger 260 behind the 325i. Early European M3s used the Getrag 265 with a dogleg first gear — first is back and left, where the racetrack wants it. Automatics were ZF three- and four-speeds.

Power reaches a differential in sizes 168 mm (small case) or 188 mm (medium case, 325i/M3), with limited-slip 25 % lock as the factory option worth hunting for.

Fahrwerk & Karosserie

Chassis

The E30 is a steel unibody with MacPherson struts up front and BMW's 15° semi-trailing arm rear axle — the geometry that gives the car its famous mid-corner chattiness. Steering is rack-and-pinion, brakes are discs front (vented on the 325i and M3) with discs or drums at the rear depending on model.

It's a car you can service on jack stands with a basic metric set — which is exactly how this one is kept alive.

Räder

Bolt pattern 4 × 100, centre bore 57.1 mm — the numbers every E30 owner knows by heart. The period-correct hierarchy runs from steel wheels with hubcaps, through the 14-inch "bottlecap" alloys, to the BBS cross-spoke baskets that define the look of the car.

Design

Formsprache

The E30 kept the forward-leaning shark nose, quad round headlights and the Hofmeister kink at the C-pillar — drawn under design chief Claus Luthe's studio with ruler-straight discipline. Chrome bumpers on early cars, body-colour plastic after the 1987 facelift; purists argue about it at every meet, and this garage politely declines to take sides in writing.
